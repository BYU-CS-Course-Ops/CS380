# Discovery Interviews — Good vs. Leading Questions (+ Consent & Ethics)

*A quick reference for Session 4 and for your own discovery conversations.*

---

## The move: from *leading* to *learning*

Leading questions invite a polite "yes" or a made-up answer. Good questions ask about **real, past behavior.**

| Instead of (leading) | Ask (learning) |
|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that does X?" | "Tell me about the last time you dealt with [problem]." |
| "Do you think this is a good idea?" | "How do you handle this today?" |
| "Wouldn't it be great if…?" | "What's the most frustrating part of…?" |
| "Do you usually…?" | "Walk me through the last time you did…" |
| "How much would you pay for this?" | "What do you currently spend (time or money) on this?" |
| "Does this problem bother you?" | "When did this last cost you time or money?" |
| "You'd want feature Y, right?" | "What have you tried to solve this? What happened?" |

## Four follow-up moves

- **"Walk me through the last time…"** — anchors to a real instance instead of a generality.
- **"Why?" / "What happened next?"** — dig past the first, surface-level answer.
- **"How do you deal with it now?"** — surfaces workarounds (proof the problem is real).
- **Silence** — let them fill it. Don't rush to talk.

## Don't

- Pitch your idea, then ask what they think.
- Treat compliments as data.
- Ask about the future ("would you…") — ask about the past.
- Do most of the talking.

---

## Consent & ethics (course norms)

You're talking to real people — treat it with care. This is **course-level needfinding, not formal human-subjects research** (no IRB needed), but the norms still apply:

- **Ask permission and say why.** *"I'm a student learning about [problem] — could I ask you a few questions? About 10 minutes."*
- **Be honest.** You're there to learn, not to sell. Don't pretend to be doing something you're not.
- **No recording without explicit permission.** Notes are fine; ask first for audio/video.
- **Respect their time and their "no."** They can stop, skip a question, or decline.
- **Protect their privacy.** Don't put names or identifying details in your notes or brief without consent — summarize and anonymize.
- **Check with the instructor first** before interviewing sensitive populations or topics (minors, health information, anything you're unsure about).
