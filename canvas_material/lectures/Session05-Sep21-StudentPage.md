## Purpose

You've spent three weeks *diverging* — generating and investigating problems. Now you start to *converge*, and the first honest question a founder asks is: **can I actually build a real slice of this with the time and skill I have — and if not yet, can I close the gap?** Today gives you the tools to answer it: how to name and weigh **feasibility risk**, how to sort what you must **know / learn / avoid**, how to run a **spike** to test the scariest unknown cheaply, and — the part most people skip — how to **plan the learning itself** instead of hoping it happens. Self-directed learning is the skill under all the other skills, and you'll use it for the rest of your career.

---

## Learning objectives

By the end of this session, you'll be able to:

1. **Name the six feasibility-risk categories** and spot which ones threaten a given idea.
2. **Triage requirements into know / learn / avoid**, and scope a **time-boxed spike** to reduce your biggest unknown.
3. **Describe how you learn** — the techniques that work for you, where you are on the learning curve, and how you'll know a gap is closed.
4. **Draft a learning plan** for a real skill gap: a *why*, goals broken down (at least one **SMART**), a schedule, an accountability partner, and something you'll **build**.
5. **Choose a tech stack deliberately**, and **use AI as a learning aid** without outsourcing your understanding.

## What we'll do together

Feasibility as named-and-reduced risk (the six categories); the know / learn / avoid triage; the **spike** — a cheap experiment that turns "is this doomed?" into a scoping decision; how you actually learn (the learning curve and the techniques that pay); and the five-step **learning plan** — all traced live through one worked example. You'll finish by running your own idea through a quick feasibility pass and writing a one-gap learning plan.
