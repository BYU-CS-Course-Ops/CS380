Interview Prompt Cards

For the mock-interview rounds. The "user" picks a real problem area they genuinely have and answers truthfully. Interviewers do not pitch — they learn.

How to use (triads — 3 rounds)

InterviewerGood, non-leading questions to understand the problem — no solutions, no pitching.
UserPick a card (page 2) you actually experience; answer from your own life.
ObserverTally leading questions and capture the real needs that surface (page 2).

Rotate roles each round so everyone is Interviewer once.

Interviewer's funnel — works for any topic; you're starting cold

  1. Open the area (~30 sec): "Tell me about [their topic] — how does that usually go for you?"
  2. Get a real, recent instance: "When did it last go wrong or frustrate you? Walk me through it."
  3. Follow what they actually do: "What do you do about it now?" — their workaround is where the hidden need lives.
  4. Ladder to the root — ask "why" 2–3 times: "Why was that the hard part?" → "And why does that matter?"
  5. Measure the pain: "How often does this happen? What does it cost you — time, money, stress?"
  6. Reflect it back: "So it sounds like the real headache is ___ — is that right?"
  7. If time: "If you could wave a wand and fix one part of this, which would it be?"

Reaching the issue they haven't named

People hand you a symptom ("group projects are stressful"), not the root problem. You reach the real, often-unspoken need by following the workaround (a pain they've quietly accepted), asking "why / what happens then" a couple of times to climb from symptom to stake, and reflecting it back until they say "yeah — exactly."

Worked example — topic the "user" picked: group projects

  • I:"Tell me about group projects — how do they usually go?"
  • U:"Honestly, stressful."
  • I:"When did one last stress you out? Walk me through it."
  • U:"Last week I ended up doing most of the slides the night before."
  • I:"How did it get to that point?"
  • U:"People said they'd do their parts and didn't — I found out too late."
  • I:"What do you do about that now?" (workaround)
  • U:"I just assume I'll have to redo things, so I over-prepare."
  • I:"Why is finding out late the hard part?" (why-ladder)
  • U:"Because by then there's no time to fix it without doing it myself."
  • I:"So the real headache is you can't tell whether the work is actually happening until it's too late to react — is that it?" (reflect back)
  • U:"Yeah, exactly. It's not the work — it's the not-knowing."

Surface complaint: "stressful group projects." The real, previously-unnamed need: visibility into whether teammates are on track, early enough to act — reached in five questions, on a topic the interviewer knew nothing about going in.
