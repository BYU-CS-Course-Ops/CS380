CS 301R · The honest retrospective

What We Said vs. What Happened

Project
Every project starts on claims: that a need is real, that a plan fits the time, that the first version will prove something. A retrospective, at work or on any project, checks those claims against what actually happened. Copy each claim from what you wrote at the time, not from memory, then point at the evidence. A claim that held, shown with evidence, was a good call. A claim that didn't, written down with what you learned, is discovery working. Neither is the better answer.

The evidence rule: every held or refuted points at something a reader could check: a demo moment, a line on an audit or test sheet, an issue or pull request, a conversation with a user. Open is an honest answer when you name what would settle it.
Read your verdicts down the column. No mix of verdicts is the right one.
Check that every verdict points at evidence, that every held was something that could have failed, and that it says why the call was right. Which row did you most want to skip? That's usually the one worth writing about.
This becomes your retrospective: the strongest rows are "what held and what didn't"; the open ones are "what the first version didn't prove." CS 301R
