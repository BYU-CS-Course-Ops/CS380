Bug Report Anatomy

The test: could a stranger reproduce it
without asking you anything?
A person's error puts a defect in the code → when it runs, the program hits a fault → you see a failure. A report describes the failure; the investigation walks the chain backward.

The six parts in the order a reader needs them

1
One failure per report. Two problems, two issues: each gets fixed, closed, and found on its own.
2
A title that summarizes the failure.
Weak: "Search broken"  Strong: "Search finds nothing when the query ends with a space"
3
Steps to reproduce, numbered and minimal: from a known start, the fewest steps that still fail.
4
Expected vs. actual, as two separate lines. "It doesn't work" is neither.
5
Environment and version: OS, browser, the commit or release, anything non-default.
6
Evidence: the exact error text, a log excerpt, a screenshot. Paste text as text so it's searchable.
Not every issue is a bug. A missing feature filed in bug language needs the right type and label, not steps. A feature request gets its own discipline: the need, the user, and what they do today instead.
