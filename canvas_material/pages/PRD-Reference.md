*A **product requirements document (PRD)** is the short, shared document that says what a product is for, who it serves, and what "done well" looks like — **before** anyone argues about how to build it. Product teams everywhere write some version of one; the format varies, but the core questions don't. Use this as a reference when you write your **Product Definition Brief (CP2)**, which is a lightweight PRD.*

---

## Why a PRD exists

A PRD is the antidote to the most expensive mistake in software: building the wrong thing well. It forces a team to agree, in writing, on the **problem and the user** so that every later decision — scope, architecture, what to cut — has a fixed point to be measured against. It is a *definition of the problem*, not a description of the solution. Solutions (architecture, screens, data models) come later, in a design document.

## The common core — what almost every PRD contains

| Section | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Problem / opportunity** | What problem are we solving, and why does it matter now? |
| **Target users** | Who specifically is this for? (Bounded — who's in, who's out.) Often expressed as personas. |
| **User needs / jobs-to-be-done** | What are those users actually trying to accomplish, and what's in their way? Stated as *needs*, not features. |
| **Goals & non-goals** | What this product will do — and, just as important, what it deliberately **won't**. |
| **Success criteria / metrics** | How we'll know it works. Specific and observable, not "users are happy." |
| **Assumptions & open questions** | What we believe but haven't confirmed, and what we still need to verify. |

Fuller PRDs may also sketch **high-level requirements** (functional and non-functional), a **scope / MVP** cut, **milestones**, and **risks** — but the six rows above are the part you can't skip.

## What a PRD is *not*

- **Not a design doc.** No architecture, screens, database schema, or technology choices. If you're describing *how* it's built, you've left the PRD.
- **Not a feature list.** Features are answers; a PRD stays on the *needs* that features will later serve.
- **Not permanent.** It's a living reference you revise as you learn more from users.
