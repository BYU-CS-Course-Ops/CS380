*These two personas are about the **same need** (commuter parking) on purpose — the difference is the *method*, not the topic. Read them side by side to see what "documented honestly" means in practice.*

## The honest persona (good)

**Label:** Commuter Cara

- **Who she is:** `[E]` An off-campus BYU student who drives to campus most days *(evidence: several r/byu threads about commuter-lot parking; my own observation of the commuter lots at 9–10 a.m.)*. `[A]` Probably a sophomore or junior with a part-time job *(assumption — not confirmed)*.
- **The need / problem:** `[E]` Finding an open spot in the commuter lots mid-morning is unpredictable and eats time *(evidence: multiple forum posts; a friend's firsthand account)*. `[A]` It sometimes makes her late to class *(assumption — plausible, not verified)*.
- **How she handles it today:** `[E]` Arrives 30–45 minutes early to "camp" for a spot, or circles the lot until one frees up *(evidence: forum comments; observation)*. `[A]` She may also check a parking app *(assumption — unconfirmed)*.
- **What it costs her:** `[E]` ~30+ minutes of buffer time a day, plus real frustration *(evidence: the tone of the forum posts)*. `[A]` The occasional ticket when she parks where she shouldn't *(assumption)*.
- **What "better" looks like:** `[A]` A heads-up on which lots still have room, or real-time availability *(assumption / hypothesis — this came from me, not from a user)*.

**Sources:** (1) r/byu discussion threads on commuter parking; (2) a review of an existing campus-parking app; (3) my own observation of the commuter lot, Sept 2026.

**Top assumptions to verify in a real conversation:**
1. Does unpredictable parking actually cause late/missed class — and how often?
2. Would real-time information change what she does?
3. How many commuters feel this daily?

**Confidence:** Medium — the *problem* is well-evidenced; the *solution direction* is still just my assumption.

## The made-up persona (what NOT to do)

**Label:** Busy Ben

Ben is a 20-year-old sophomore in computer science with a 3.8 GPA. He works 15 hours a week, loves productivity apps, and is always on the go. He's constantly frustrated that he can *never* find parking and wastes about an hour every single day circling the lots. He would **definitely pay $10/month** for an app that shows open spots in real time, and he'd tell all his friends about it.

*(No sources. Nothing tagged. Every detail is invented and stated as fact.)*

---

## What to point out

- **The bad one reads great and feels "done."** That's exactly the trap — a polished character *feels* like research.
- **Every "fact" in Ben is invented and unsourced:** the 3.8 GPA, "an hour every day," "definitely pay $10/month." None of it came from a real person, and nothing is marked as a guess.
- **"Would definitely pay $10/month" is a hypothetical dressed as fact** — the exact <course-link type="page" id="pg-mom-test-summary">Mom Test</course-link> trap. Future promises aren't evidence.
- **Cara is less tidy but honest.** You can see precisely what's *evidence* vs. *assumption*, where each claim came from, and what you still need to verify.
- **Bottom line:** a founder who builds on Ben is building confidently on fiction. Cara tells you what you actually know — and what to go find out in a real conversation.
