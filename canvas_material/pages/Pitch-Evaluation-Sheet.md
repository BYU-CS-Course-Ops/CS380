## 2. The recruiting question — answer honestly

**Would you join this project?** ☐ **Yes** ☐ **Maybe** ☐ **No**

**Why — one line:** _______________________________________________

*Answer as if you had no project of your own — see the note below.*

*"Yes" means you would actually consider spending your semester on it. It is a signal, not a compliment — and "No" with high scores above is a perfectly consistent answer (a strong idea that isn't for you).*

**Answer as an outside observer.** You have your own project, and so does everyone else in the room — which is exactly why *"I already have my own idea"* is not an answer to this question. Answer the version that carries information: **if you walked in today with no project of your own, looking for something worth a semester, would this be it?** Checking "Yes" commits you to nothing; teams form after the instructor blends everyone's input. But a room that all answers defensively hands the selection no signal at all, and tells every presenter only that their classmates were busy.

*A useful check on your own answer: if your **why** line is about **you** ("I'm already committed"), the bias is talking. If it's about the **project** ("the domain doesn't interest me," "I don't believe the scope," "I'd have nothing to build there"), it's information.*

*The **why** matters more to the presenter than the checkbox. "The domain doesn't interest me, but the project is sound" and "I don't believe the scope" are the same "no" and completely different information. Write the reason you'd want to be given.*

### Scoring notes (from the Session 9 calibration)

- **Score the criteria independently.** A pitch can be a 3 on value and a 1 on scope realism — that's exactly the kind of signal the selection needs.
- **Feasibility ≠ enthusiasm.** If the demo of confidence exceeded the evidence, score the evidence.
- **Don't grade on a curve in your head.** Each pitch against the criteria, not against the previous pitch.
- **Comments are for the presenter.** Write the sentence you'd want to receive: specific, usable, kind.
