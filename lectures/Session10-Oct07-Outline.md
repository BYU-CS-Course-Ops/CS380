> **Design decision (Tom, Aug 2026) — evaluation happens today, commitment happens Monday.** Pitch Day used to end with students physically sorting into teams in the last ten minutes. It doesn't any more, for two reasons. **Coherence:** Sessions 2, 3, and 6 teach deferring judgment and converging deliberately against criteria — then asking students to make the semester's biggest convergence in ten minutes, on their feet, in front of everyone, contradicts all of it. **Evidence:** the written proposals already exist (CP3, submitted Oct 5), and scope realism, richness, and stack are far better judged from the document than from six spoken minutes. So today ends with **anchor ideas announced and private ranked preferences submitted in Canvas**; the proposals and tallies go up over the weekend; **teams are announced at the start of Session 11** and charters are written in that class. Nothing about CP4 changes — persuasion and recruiting are still measured live, by the "would you join" counts collected on the day.

## Purpose of this session

Everything from Weeks 1–4 lands here. Each student delivers **Pitch #1** — defending their idea and recruiting classmates — while the room evaluates every pitch on the shared criteria. Then the blended selection (peer scores + recruiting interest + instructor final say) surfaces the **anchor ideas**, and every student records a **private ranked preference** before leaving. The founder's first real audience is this room; how you present and how you evaluate are graded acts of professional judgment.

**Choosing is not the same as evaluating, and it doesn't happen at the same speed.** You'll evaluate live today, because a pitch is a live event and enthusiasm in the moment is real signal. You'll *commit* on Monday, after a weekend with the actual proposals in front of you — the same discipline this course has asked for since Week 3: diverge, let it sit, then converge against criteria rather than against whoever is standing nearest.

---

## Learning objectives

By the end of this session, a student can:

1. **Deliver a persuasive technical pitch** under time pressure and handle live questions.
2. **Evaluate peers' pitches** consistently against shared criteria.
3. **Record a considered preference** — rank the projects they'd join, with a reason tied to the criteria rather than to who their friends are, and revise it once they've read the documents.
4. **Separate evaluating from committing** — recognize why the two want different amounts of time, and defend a choice made with the documents in hand.

## Timed outline (≈75 min, scaled to ~8 pitches — adjust to enrollment)

### 1. Opening — run of show + calibration — 4 min
- The two jobs today: pitch well, evaluate honestly. Remind the room of the scoring anchors and the recruiting question.
- Ground rules: hard time limits (visible timer), score during the Q&A of each pitch, no score-comparing chatter between pitches.

### 2. Pitch block — ~50 min
- Each pitch: **6 min pitch + 2 min Q&A + ~30 sec transition/score-finalize.**
- Instructor asks one question per pitch if the room doesn't; keep Q&A honest but kind.
- Evaluators: score all criteria **plus** "would you join? (yes/maybe/no)" **with a one-line why**, before the next pitch starts.
- **Restate the outside-observer rule from Session 9 before the first pitch** — answer as if you had no project of your own and were looking for something worth a semester. *"I have my own idea"* is not an answer; everyone does, and a room that all answers that way hands the selection nothing.
- *(Enrollment note: at ~8 pitches this fits; above that, trim Q&A to 1 min or split the block — decide in Session 9, announce in advance.)*

### 3. Selection huddle — 8 min
- Collect sheets; tally fast at the front of the room (this is why the mechanics were settled Monday).
- Instructor reviews: peer scores + recruiting interest ("yes" counts) + own judgment → announce the **anchor ideas** (the set that can support teams of ~2–4, plus approved solo paths).
- Frame it right, and say it plainly: ideas not selected **didn't lose.** Their founders now choose which anchor to join, bringing everything they learned in discovery — and nobody's idea gets killed out from under them, since a determined founder may elect the solo path.
- **Anyone whose idea drew no interest hears it from you, here, today** — briefly and in person, not by inference from a list on the LMS over the weekend. That conversation is the reason selection stays in the room even though commitment doesn't.

### 4. Preferences — 7 min
- Hand out the **Team Preference Worksheet** (one per student). Quiet, individual, and **private — nobody sees anyone else's.** Paper first, on purpose: it's faster to think on, and the room is quiet while the pitches are fresh.
- Each student writes: **first, second, and third choice** among the anchor ideas · whether they'd rather **continue solo** (and at what scope) · **one line per choice** on why, in criteria language · one line on **what they'd bring** to that team.
- Say why it's ranked rather than binary: ranked preferences let the instructor honor almost everyone's first or second choice, where a show of hands only records where people happened to be standing.
- **Submission is the Canvas assignment, not the paper** — students copy their answers in before the end of the day. The worksheet is theirs to keep.
- **Canvas stays open until Sunday 11:59pm and submissions are editable**, so a student who changes their mind after reading the proposals just edits — no email, no negotiation, and no signal to anyone else that they changed.
- **Say the deadline twice, and check the Canvas submission list Thursday morning** — a missing preference is the one thing that stalls Monday's announcement.

### 5. What happens next + wrap — 3 min
- **By Thursday evening**, on Canvas: the anchor ideas, the tallied scores, the anonymized "would you join" comments for your own pitch, and the **full written proposals for every anchor project.**
- **Over the weekend: read them.** Six minutes of pitch tells you whether someone can present; the proposal tells you whether the scope is real, whether the stack is one you want to work in, and whether there's something in it you'd want to build. Judge on the document.
- **Recruiting between now and Monday is fine and normal** — founders recruit outside meetings. Two rules: the instructor holds final say, and nobody gets pressured. If a conversation feels like pressure, it is; say so.
- **Mon, Oct 12:** teams announced at the start of class; **charters written in class** and submitted by end of day. Also Monday: Phase 3 begins — **read the assigned design doc before class** (see the Session 11 page).
