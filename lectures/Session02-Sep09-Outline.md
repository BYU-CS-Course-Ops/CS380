## Purpose of this session

Two jobs, in order:

1. **Teach the CPS process hands-on** — walk the four stages and the divergent/convergent distinction through a concrete, low-stakes exercise (the egg-drop), so the method is felt, not just heard.
2. **Turn that engine on the real task** — generate a large, *wide* pool of candidate problems, needs, and users, deferring judgment, and pour it into the idea journals. This is the raw material for the Idea Briefs (CP1, due Sep 23).

The measure of a good session: students leave with far more candidate problems than they walked in with, and a few that genuinely tug at them.

---

## Learning objectives

By the end of Session 2, a student can:

1. **Describe the four CPS stages** and apply divergent, then convergent, thinking to a concrete problem.
2. **Practice deferring judgment** — generate many ideas before evaluating any.
3. **Use 2–3 divergent tools** (post-it brainstorming, brainwriting, "how might we…", ladder of abstraction) to explore a problem space.
4. **Generate a broad set of candidate problems** framed around *real users and needs* — not solutions.
5. **Leave with a materially expanded idea journal** and 3–5 starred problem spaces to investigate.

## Timed outline (≈75 min)

### 1. Bridge & idea-journal check-in — 5 min
- Recap the CPS vocabulary from Session 1 (divergent vs. convergent) in one breath.
- Quick pulse: *how many entries in your journal so far?* Hands up by range. Celebrate volume — reinforce that quantity is the point right now.
- Frame today: first we practice the process, then we use it to find real problems.

### 2. CPS + creativity — from the readings — 13 min

**(a) Consolidate CPS (~4 min).** They've read the handbook, so *consolidate* rather than lecture: the four stages — **Clarification → Ideation → Development → Implementation** — and where divergent and convergent thinking live in each. Pull points from the reading through discussion, not slides.

**(b) Discuss "Channeling Your Creativity" (~5 min).** Everyone read Elder Hales's article. Run a short discussion:
- *What does the article add beyond the CPS mechanics?* (creativity as a gift to develop and use; inviting inspiration; the moral / consecration dimension — creating to serve and bless, not just to produce.)
- *What difference might that make in how you approach finding a project worth founding?*
- Tie it to the course's through-line: we're not just generating ideas — we're looking for real needs worth serving. Keep it warm and authentic, not heavy-handed.

**(c) The tools we'll use today (~4 min) — introduce, don't drill.** Name the handful of tools the workshop will use so the moves are familiar when we start. *(Make this a slide too — a list with one-line summaries, mirroring the table below.)*

| Tool | Mode | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Post-It brainstorming** | Diverge | Rapid capture — one idea per sticky, say it aloud, go for quantity, no judging. |
| **Brainwriting** | Diverge | Write a few ideas on a sheet, swap, and build on someone else's — silent, so no one dominates. |
| **"How might we…?"** | Diverge | Reframe a gripe or problem as an open, invitational question that invites many answers. |
| **Webbing / Ladder of Abstraction** | Both | Ask *"what's stopping you?"* to get more specific, or *"why does it matter?"* to go broader — move a need up or down. |
| **Value vs. Do-ability** | Converge | Score options on two axes — how valuable × how feasible — to narrow a big list without discarding novelty. |

- *To flesh out: keep the Hales discussion brief; decide how explicitly to invoke the spiritual dimension vs. letting it surface. The tools slide mirrors this table.*

### 3. Egg-drop CPS exercise (paper only) — 14 min
- **Paper only — no build, no drop.** This is a *demonstration of the process*, not a real test: teams design and reason on paper. Groups of 3–4, a facilitator each; expect interruptions and feedback.
- **Run it as: ideate first, constraints later.** Start with a light Clarification (the goal only — "protect an egg dropped from increasing heights") and go straight to **Ideation**: generate many protection ideas, defer judgment, go wild. *Don't hand out constraints yet.*
- **Reveal the constraints at Development** (set below) and have teams **converge** — evaluate their wild ideas against the constraints and narrow to the one they'd build. This makes divergent → convergent visceral: the fun ideas meet real limits.
- **The Clarification twist:** hold the constraints ready and reveal them *early to any team that asks the right questions during Clarification* ("what materials do we have? how many drops? what counts as success? any size limit?"). Teams that probe get to ideate *with* the constraints in mind; teams that don't have to re-narrow at Development. That contrast *is* the lesson about why Clarification matters — so keep the set ready but don't volunteer it.

**Constraint set — hold ready; reveal at Development, or on request during Clarification:**

- **Materials kit (only these):** 1 sheet of paper · 12 in of tape · 5 drinking straws · 8 in of string · 10 cotton balls · 2 rubber bands · 1 sandwich bag. Nothing else.
- **Size:** the finished device must fit inside an **8-inch cube.**
- **Weight:** the device (without the egg) must be **under 2 oz.**
- **Build time:** assembled in **≤ 10 minutes.**
- **Reusable:** must protect the egg across **three increasing drops (3 ft → 6 ft → 9 ft)** — no one-shot designs.
- **Egg access:** the egg must go in and come out **without cutting or destroying** the device.
- **Success:** egg **uncracked after the 9 ft drop**, within every limit above. Tiebreaker: **fewer materials and lighter weight score higher.**

- *To flesh out: tune the kit and limits to taste — they're set to force real tradeoffs (a giant pillow or parachute blows the size/weight/materials limits), which is what makes the convergence step actually bite.*

### 4. Debrief → transfer to problem-finding — 6 min
- Quick share: which tools helped? what were the wildest ideas, and did they lead anywhere? where did divergent vs. convergent show up?
- **Name the Clarification lesson:** the teams that asked about constraints early had an easier time — that's exactly why real founding work starts by understanding the problem, its users, and its constraints before racing to solutions.
- **The pivot:** the same divergent discipline is exactly how we'll *find problems worth founding on* — generate widely, defer judgment. Now we point it at the real world.

### 5. Divergent problem-space workshop — the main event — 26 min
- **The task:** generate a large pool of **problems and needs people around you actually have — not solutions.** Everything goes on the wall and into journals; nothing is judged yet.
- **Groups are fluid** (~3–4, reshuffled from the egg-drop). These are **not** future teams — teams don't form until Week 5 — so mix freely.
- **Keep the rules visible the whole time.** This is a **persistent slide** left up during the workshop: *defer judgment · prefer quantity · build on others' · welcome wild · no solutioning yet.*
- **Set a target to keep generation flowing:** e.g., "each group, aim for 40+ problem notes by the end." All three tools below are still *generating* — just by different mechanisms — and they all feed the **same growing pool** (don't cull between phases).

**Suggested sequence — breadth → build → depth:**

1. **Frame + set the task (~1 min).** Put up the rules slide and the target; groups to their wall space.
2. **Post-It burst — breadth (~9 min).** Individual, loud, fast: each person writes problems on sticky notes (one per note), says it aloud, and slaps it on the group's wall. Start from your idea journal, then push past the obvious. Phrase them openly where you can ("how might we…" / "how might a [user]…"). *This is the energy engine — it fills the wall quickly.*
3. **Brainwriting — build (~9 min).** Shift to silent building so quiet voices count: on a printed brainwriting sheet, each person writes a few problem statements, then passes the sheet; the next person **builds on or adds adjacent problems** to what's already there. Swap a few times. New problems keep coming — now sparked by others' notes rather than a blank page.
4. **Ladder of Abstraction — depth (~7 min).** Quick **whole-class demo first (~2 min):** grab a real problem note off one of the groups' walls and ladder it live — ask *"why does this matter?"* to climb to the bigger need, and *"what's stopping them / what specifically?"* to drop to concrete sub-problems. Then each group picks **2–3 promising notes and ladders them (~5 min)**, spinning off a cluster of related, better-framed problems around a real need.

- **Light "real users" prompt throughout (the deep dive is Week 2):** as notes go up, nudge students to tag **who** feels each problem — a person or a group, not "everyone" — as a one-word tag. Real needfinding and interviews are next week's topic; today it just keeps the pool grounded in people. The ladder step naturally surfaces the user ("why does it matter — and to whom?").
- **Lenses to spark breadth** (ready if a group stalls): your clubs, job, major, ward, roommates, hobbies; groups you belong to; things that take too many steps; communities that are underserved; tools you wish existed.
- **Alternative ordering for a quiet room:** run **brainwriting first** (silent, low social pressure, everyone contributes) → then the **Post-It burst** (louder, building on a room that's already warmed up) → **ladder**. Same three tools, gentler on-ramp.
- *To flesh out: final timeboxes.*

### 6. Light convergence + capture — 6 min
- Each student **privately stars 3–5 problem spaces** that tug at them — a first, gentle convergence on *value × reachable users*. This is not a commitment; it's a shortlist to investigate.
- Everything — starred or not — goes into the idea journal before they leave.

### 7. Wrap + before next class — 5 min
- Reinforce: keep feeding the journal all week — you need real range to write **three genuinely different** Idea Briefs.
- Preview **Week 2 (Mon, Sep 14):** open source, licensing, and **discovery methods** — how to talk to real users and build honest personas. Bring your starred problem spaces.
- *To flesh out: assign Week-2 pre-reading.*
