## The short version

Deliver a **6–8 minute pitch + 2–3 minutes of Q&A** that does two things at once: **defends** your project (real need, feasible scope) and **recruits** classmates to build it with you. The room scores every pitch on the shared criteria; teams form the same day around the ideas that persuade.

---

## Why this assignment exists

Ideas don't attract collaborators by being good; they attract collaborators by being *communicated* well enough that someone else can see themselves in the work. This is the founder's recruiting problem, and it never goes away — it's how open-source maintainers attract contributors, how startup founders attract cofounders, how engineers inside companies get headcount for the thing they believe in. The pitch is where you practice it with real stakes: your classmates are genuinely deciding how to spend their semester, and their choice is part of your grade.

The deeper skill is **thinking on your feet about your own project's weaknesses.** Anyone can present rehearsed strengths; the Q&A tests whether you can be honest under pressure — and "I don't know yet, here's how I'd find out" delivered calmly is one of the most trust-building sentences in engineering. Oral technical communication, persuasion with evidence, and composure under questioning are graded here because they're graded everywhere: design reviews, conference talks, job interviews, funding meetings, and your own Demo Day in December.

---

## What to produce

**The pitch (6–8 min)** — the Session 9 arc, adapted to your material:

1. **The problem & who has it** (~90 sec) — lead with the user and your evidence, not the app.
2. **The solution** (~90 sec) — what you'd build; the vertical slice, concretely.
3. **Why it's feasible** (~2 min) — scope, stack, your top risk and its mitigation. Honest confidence.
4. **Why it's worth joining** (~90 sec) — richness: what a teammate would actually get to build; where it grows.
5. **The ask** (~30 sec) — explicit: *"I'm looking for 1–3 teammates; here's what we'd do first."*

**Slides:** ~5–7, nothing under ~30pt, one idea per slide (Kawasaki rules apply). Posted/loaded **before class** — no day-of fiddling.

**Q&A (2–3 min):** answer what's asked; never bluff a feasibility question.

**As an evaluator:** you also score every other pitch on the shared sheet (the selection criteria + "would you join?"). Honest scoring is part of your professionalism grade in this course's culture — and it feeds the selection. It's the same idea-evaluation skill you used on your own candidates in Week 3, now aimed at your peers'.

## Requirements & constraints

- **Hard time limit.** The timer is visible and enforced; running long costs you under Delivery.
- **Individual.** This is your idea's moment; teams don't exist yet.
- **A pitch that doesn't recruit is not a failure** — solo paths are allowed, and strong ideas sometimes anchor teams via selection anyway. But the ask must still be made.
- **Rehearse out loud, against a timer, at least twice.** Reading slides aloud is not a pitch.

## Grading

Scored on a 100-point analytic rubric; **8% of the course grade.** Peer scores inform the instructor's judgment; the grade is the instructor's.

| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem/solution clarity** | 20 | The audience grasps the need and the idea immediately — no rewinding. |
| **Persuasiveness & recruiting** | 25 | Compelling enough to attract teammates, with a clear call to join. Weighted highest because the pitch's *job* is to recruit — audience effect is part of the grade. |
| **Feasibility defense** | 15 | Scope and tradeoffs defended convincingly — the CP3 honest-confidence move, spoken. |

**Performance levels:** Exemplary (4) · Proficient (3) · Developing (2) · Beginning (1) · Absent (0). Score per criterion = (level ÷ 4) × weight.

## Tips and common pitfalls

- **The classic failure:** five minutes on the problem, thirty seconds on feasibility. Time-box each beat and cut until it fits.
- **Pitch the work, not just the product.** "You'd get to build the scheduling engine and the data layer" recruits engineers; a feature list doesn't.
- **Prep the three likely questions:** *Why would anyone use this over X? What's your riskiest assumption? What gets cut first if you're behind at Week 10?*
- **If the demo of your enthusiasm exceeds the evidence, the room notices.** Calibrate.
- **Score others the way you'd want scoring:** honestly, on the criteria, with the recruiting question answered for real.
