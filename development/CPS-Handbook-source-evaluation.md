# CPS Handbook — reading-source evaluation

> **Instructor document — not a student handout.** This is the decision record for which CPS reading the course assigns and why. Students get the handbook itself (`resources/cps_handbook.pdf`) plus the assigned page range, posted to the LMS.

*For the Session 2 (Wed, Sep 9) pre-reading. Students will have had no prior exposure to Creative Problem Solving, so the reading needs to be clear, self-contained, and focused on the divergent/convergent thinking we emphasize.*

---

## Recommendation (primary)

**"Creative Problem Solving — a quick, down-and-dirty handbook"** — Knowinnovation & Inclusive Innovation (© 2012–2017). This is the same handbook the 2025–2026 course linked (hosted on a Berkeley site).

- **Local copy (use this):** `resources/cps_handbook.pdf` — saved in this folder for the LMS.
- *Original source:* https://brdo.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/cps_handbook.pdf
- **What it is:** a 33-page practitioner handbook on the Osborn–Parnes CPS process.

**Why it's the right pick for us — it's tightly aligned with what we already teach.** The divergent/convergent principles and the specific tools named in our Session 1 and Session 2 outlines come straight from this document:

- the **five principles of divergent thinking** (defer judgment, go for quantity, seek wild ideas, build on others', write everything down) and the **four principles of convergent thinking** — laid out cleanly on two facing pages;
- the **four stages** (Clarification → Ideation → Development → Implementation), matching our CPS→phases slide exactly;
- the exact **tools** we reference — **Post-It brainstorming, Brainwriting, Webbing / Ladder of Abstraction, "How might we…" question stems, and Value vs. Do-ability.**

It's also free, openly accessible, and genuinely readable (its "down-and-dirty" tone is a feature for undergrads with no prior exposure).

**One caveat to flag for students:** the handbook is written for *facilitated group innovation sessions*, so it spends a few pages on roles (Problem Owner, Facilitator, Resource Group) that matter less to an individual student doing problem-finding. Tell them to read past the facilitation framing and focus on the *thinking process and tools*.

### Suggested assignment — a focused subset (~12–15 pages of real reading)

Rather than all 33 pages, assign the core and let them skim the rest:

- **Read closely:** pp. 1 & 3 (what CPS is + the one-page principles summary), **pp. 6–10 (the divergent/convergent "dynamic balance," the divergent & convergent principles, deferring judgment, and the four-stage overview — this is the heart of it).**
- **Skim:** pp. 11–16 (the per-stage detail).
- **Read the tool write-ups we'll actually use:** "Posing Problems as Questions" and "Webbing / Ladder of Abstraction" (pp. 17–18), "Post-It Brainstorming" and "Brainwriting" (p. 19), and **"Value vs. Do-ability" (p. 27).**
- **Skip:** the roles/facilitation pages (pp. 4–5) and the closing pages about Knowinnovation (pp. 29–33).

This keeps the load reasonable for a first pre-reading while covering everything Session 2 builds on.

## Alternatives considered

- **Mitchell & Kowalik, "Creative Problem Solving"** (an older academic CPS text). More thorough and scholarly, and it treats the Osborn–Parnes model in depth — but it's longer, drier, and the only free copy I found is on an unstable mirror (geocities.ws), so link durability is a risk. *Consider only if you specifically want a more academic source.*
- **Creative Education Foundation (CEF)** — the organization Osborn founded, which stewards the CPS model today (https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org). Good authoritative background and an official "CPS process" description; useful as a reference/citation, but not packaged as a single tidy student reading.
- **Short web primers** (e.g., Project Bliss, MindTools articles on the Osborn–Parnes process). Fine as a lightweight optional warm-up, but too thin to be the main reading.

**Bottom line:** the Knowinnovation handbook is the best fit — it *is* the source our sessions are built on. The main improvement over "assign the whole PDF" is assigning the focused subset above.
